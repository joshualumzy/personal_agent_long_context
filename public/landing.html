At a small company, the answer already exists. Finding it is the job.

It sits in a postmortem, a Slack thread and one colleague's memory. People rebuild it by hand before every call, then write the follow-up from memory after. Promises slip, and the people who know the system get stretched thinner.

One Tuesday with Kaki

At 11, Jax meets a customer the team just let down. Here is how the day goes.

  1. 09:00

    Homework, with receipts

    Jax asks if ENG-210 is the same bug as ENG-148. Kaki reads the tickets, postmortems and chats, and every claim opens the record it came from. Pick a past date and it answers from what was known that day.

  2. 11:00

    On the call, it keeps track

    Kaki writes the call down line by line. When someone says plan A after the team agreed on B, it flags the conflict. When someone reads out a key, that line is withheld before any model sees it.

  3. 12:00

    Promises become drafts

    When the call ends, each promise is a draft email, ticket or meeting, and each shows whose words it came from. Approve, and your own Gmail opens with it filled in. You press send. The 20% discount goes to Finance.

  4. 16:00

    It sees the gap early

    Kaki tracks who knows what. When part of the system is left with too few people, it proposes a hire, then helps you go from criteria to candidates to an outreach draft.

Kaki suggests. You decide.

Every answer shows its sources

Kaki checks each citation against the records it actually read in that run. If the records don't support an answer, it says so instead of guessing.

CONF-ENG-311 “The same code path was implicated in ENG-148; the prior fix only addressed a specific edge case.”

Nothing sends itself

Approving opens your own Gmail, calendar or tracker with the draft filled in. The last click is always yours.

Edit it, approve it again

Approval belongs to the exact draft you read. Change a word and it asks again.

Money goes to its owner

Discounts and contract terms are passed to the person who can approve them, not to you and not to Kaki.

Secrets never reach the model

Keys, personal IDs and planted instructions in a transcript are held back before any model reads them.
